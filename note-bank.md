Subject: UK Banking Adverse News Weekly | 21–27 September 2026

Please find below the key adverse developments relevant to the UK banking sector for the week 21–27 September 2026, covering operational and cybersecurity risk, regulatory enforcement and legal risk, financial crime and compliance, and prudential and financial stability.

"Trust relationships becoming the attack surface" — regulators, former suppliers, APIs and authenticated communications rather than the bank's own perimeter.

KEY ADVERSE DEVELOPMENTS

1. OPERATIONAL & CYBERSECURITY RISKS

• Revolut – Italian Government Email Compromise (case update from last week):
Further developments followed the compromise of an official Italian government certified email account (entilocali.prefrc@pec.interno.it) used to submit fraudulent law-enforcement information requests to Revolut. Approximately 680 customers were affected, with identity and transaction-related information disclosed. Italian authorities widened scrutiny to establish whether similar fraudulent requests had been directed at other financial institutions.
Source: Italian Data Protection Authority / Italian reporting on the Revolut incident.

• Revolut / DriveWealth – Third-Party Data Breach:
On 24 September, Revolut customers were confirmed as affected by a separate security incident at US brokerage provider DriveWealth. Unauthorised access exposed historical customer profile and biographical data, including names, contact information, employment details, citizenship information and partial account numbers. Revolut stated that its own systems and infrastructure were not compromised.
Source: RTÉ, 24 September 2026.
https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/0924/1592820-revolut-data/

2. REGULATORY ENFORCEMENT & LEGAL RISKS

• FCA – CFD Firms:
On 25 September, the FCA announced that 24 CFD firms had closed or were cancelling their permissions following a crackdown on misuse of UK regulatory authorisation. The FCA found that some firms carrying out limited UK business were using their authorised status to lend credibility to linked overseas entities, potentially misleading customers about the protections available to them.
Source: FCA, 25 September 2026.
https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/twenty-four-cfd-firms-closing-crackdown-misuse-uk-authorisation

3. FINANCIAL CRIME & COMPLIANCE RISKS

• UK Banking Sector – Money Mule Accounts:
On 23 September, the FCA reported that regulated firms closed 238,396 suspected money-mule accounts in 2025, compared with 233,269 in 2024 and 184,935 in 2023. The FCA said organised criminal groups continue to move illicit funds through networks of bank accounts before cashing out.
Source: FCA, 23 September 2026.
https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/firms-crack-down-money-mules-need-do-more

4. PRUDENTIAL & FINANCIAL STABILITY

• Bank of England – Small and Mid-Sized Bank Resolution:
On 23 September, the Bank of England outlined its evolving approach to resolving small and medium-sized banks. The Bank emphasised that resolution strategy should not be determined solely by balance-sheet size and highlighted the need to retain transfer options where insolvency could create wider disruption to customers or financial stability.
Source: Bank of England, 23 September 2026.
