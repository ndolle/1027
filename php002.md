# 🚨 PHP2Shell — Alert: First Successful Exploitation Confirmed by Sophos

22 October 2026 | 19:58 BST | Threat Level: CRITICAL

Significant development: attacks have progressed from exploitation attempts to a confirmed successful exploit involving the execution of a malicious payload.

In an update published on 22 October at 16:18 UTC (17:18 BST), [Sophos Counter Threat Unit](https://www.sophos.com/en-us/blog/proof-of-concept-exploit-released-for-php-vulnerabilities-109618-109619?utm_source=chatgpt.com) confirmed that it had identified several attempts to exploit PHP2Shell (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619), including one successful exploitation.

This represents a significant change from the previous assessment, which had not established a successful compromise.

## 1. Compromise and Malware Execution

Sophos researchers described malicious activity originating from IP address `45.198.224.169`.

The attack resulted in a malicious payload being written to `/tmp/nfs`, followed by its execution using the command `./nfs php`.

The malware appears to belong to the Mirai family, specifically the Potassium variant previously mentioned in ELLIO's observations.

Confirmed facts: Sophos reports successful exploitation and execution of a malicious payload.

Limitations: No affected organisation has been publicly identified. At this stage, Sophos has not reported evidence of ransomware deployment, data exfiltration or supply-chain compromise.

## 3. Operational Implications

### SOC — Immediate Priority

Search for the new IoCs retrospectively from 17 October, extending the investigation back to 1 August where telemetry is available.

Correlate HTTP, EDR and DNS events, particularly:

- File creation activity in `/tmp`.
- Unusual process execution originating from PHP.
- Outbound connections to the identified malicious infrastructure.

A network IoC match alone does not demonstrate a compromise. However, unauthorised execution originating from a PHP process should trigger a priority incident response investigation.

### Vulnerability Management — Critical Priority

Maintain immediate remediation of exposed PHP installations.

Verify that security patches are effectively applied to running services, including vendor backports and containerised environments.

### Second Line of Defence (2LoD) — Risk Reassessment

This new observation justifies treating the compromise risk as materialised in observed threat activity, rather than purely theoretical.

For critical assets, the First Line of Defence (1LoD) should provide documented confirmation of:

- Threat-hunting coverage.
- Investigation findings and conclusions.
- Remediation status and supporting evidence.

Applying security patches alone is insufficient to rule out a compromise that may have occurred before remediation.

This development is more significant than previous announcements concerning new detection signatures or exploitation attempts.

This reinforces the need to conduct retrospective compromise assessments, including on systems that have already been patched.
