# Banking sector: credit-intelligence findings
**Research cutoff: 27 October 2026 | Scope: worldwide, with EU, UK and US comparisons**

## 1. Executive assessment


**The EU, UK and US evidence supports a resilient aggregate banking-sector baseline, rather than a uniform assessment of individual banks.** The EBA’s Q2 2026 dashboard describes robust capital and liquidity buffers; the Bank of England’s July assessment describes an appropriately capitalised, liquid banking system; and the FDIC reports strong capital and liquidity alongside improved Q2 earnings and asset-quality metrics. These publications cover different populations and observation periods. ([eba.europa.eu](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eueea-banks-display-strength-amid-challenging-risk-environment?utm_source=openai))



**Credit assessment — moderate confidence:** aggregate resilience is a useful starting point, but does not establish counterparty creditworthiness. The principal differentiators are recurring earnings, deposit stability, borrower and sector concentrations, capital headroom, nonbank interconnectedness, and operational dependencies. A single worldwide credit-risk rating is not justified by the evidence assembled.

## 3. Credit transmission channels

### Earnings quality and loss absorption


**US bank-level illustration — company disclosure, high confidence:** Zions’ Q2 2026 release, published **20 July 2026**, reported net earnings of **$452 million**, including net equity investment gains of **$215 million on Visa shares** and **$37 million on SBIC investments**. It reported CET1 of **11.8%** and annualised net charge-offs of **0.06%**. These are institution-specific figures, not US-sector estimates. [3](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281611158064463897)



**Assessment:** recurring pre-provision earnings should be distinguished from investment gains and other exceptional items when evaluating sustainable loss-absorption capacity. Strong headline earnings alone do not establish stronger underlying credit quality.


### Borrower stress and nonbank interconnectedness


**UK/global linkages — supervisory assessment, high confidence:** the July 2026 Financial Stability Report identifies vulnerabilities involving private credit, leveraged borrowers and bank–nonbank interconnections. It highlights prime-broker exposures and significant risk transfers as relevant transmission channels. These are identified vulnerabilities, not forecasts of a specified loss. ([bankofengland.co.uk](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability-report/2026/july-2026?utm_source=openai))



**Assessment — moderate confidence:** counterparty analysis should consider funded loans, undrawn facilities, derivatives, collateral and shared borrowers together. Direct loan balances alone may not capture exposure to fund financing, market illiquidity or correlated collateral deterioration.


### Operational, cyber and financial-crime risk


**UK — primary regulatory evidence, high confidence:** the FCA’s **28 July 2026** publication states that **27% of incidents reported by firms in 2025 were attributed to a third-party issue**, and **37% of those third-party incidents were cyber-related**. The population includes financial firms beyond banks; the statistics are neither banking-only nor 2026 incident rates. The FCA identifies concentration in commonly used technology, cloud and data providers as a system-wide resilience concern. [4](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281628059314421762)



**Europe — credit-agency assessment, moderate confidence:** commentary published **12 August 2026** identifies fines, operational disruption and reputational damage as channels through which AML/CFT deficiencies can affect bank credit profiles. It identifies governance, due diligence, monitoring and resourcing as recurring control concerns; this is not an allegation against every European bank. [5](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281662835949830161)


## 4. Subsector and business-model implications


The following are **analytical assessment frameworks**, not verified findings about every institution in each category.

| Subsector / model | Credit-relevant differentiation |
|---|---|
| **Universal banks** | Diversification must be weighed against cross-border complexity, market exposures and intragroup dependencies. Assess segment-level earnings and capital transferability. |
| **Retail, commercial and regional banks** | Examine deposit concentration, repricing, mortgage/CRE/SME concentrations, securities valuations and refinancing needs. |
| **Investment banks and prime brokers** | Focus on counterparty leverage, collateral, margin liquidity, underwriting commitments and stressed market liquidity. |
| **Custody and transaction banks** | Assess settlement continuity, intraday liquidity, operational concentration and sensitivity of fee income and deposits to client activity. |
| **Digital banks and neobanks** | Distinguish licensed deposit-taking banks from payment or e-money models. Assess deposit behaviour, recurring profitability, cloud dependencies and recovery capability. |
| **Specialist and consumer lenders** | Examine underwriting vintages, arrears migration, borrower affordability, wholesale/securitisation funding and product concentration. |
| **Cooperative, mutual and public-policy banks** | Assess geographic concentration, governance, group support arrangements and any explicit support mechanism; do not assume sovereign backing. |

Confidence in applying this framework is **moderate**; issuer-level conclusions require institution-specific disclosures.


## 5. Evidence gaps and limits


**Incident evidence remains qualified.** Reporting published **22 October 2026** describes a Google Cloud disruption affecting Revolut services and quotes company assurances that funds and data remained safe. This is secondary reporting of company statements, not a primary incident investigation. The retrieved evidence does not establish an attacker, exploited weakness or quantified bank loss. **Confidence: moderate for the reported disruption; unresolved for root cause and financial impact.** [6](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281825420124880906)



The following gaps limit report conclusions:

- **Worldwide coverage is incomplete:** current, comparable primary evidence was not assembled for China, Japan, India, Latin America or most African banking systems.
- **Latest issuer reporting is incomplete:** Q3 2026 results were not comprehensively reconciled across banks. Earlier figures are dated observations, not assurances of current condition.
- **Exposure detail is insufficient:** uninsured deposits, CRE subsegments, sovereign exposures, private-credit commitments and collateral concentrations lack a harmonised cross-region dataset.
- **Subsector evidence is uneven:** specialist lenders, custody banks, mutuals and neobanks require dedicated financial-disclosure review.
- **Operational loss evidence is insufficient:** service disruption, data compromise, fraud loss, remediation cost and regulatory penalty must remain separate categories.
- **No unverified attacker allegation is adopted as fact.** No sector-wide loss estimate, exploited weakness or attack attribution is assigned.

**Report-ready conclusion:** available evidence supports aggregate resilience in the principal developed-market banking systems, while leaving material institution-level and geographic uncertainty. Credit differentiation should rest on recurring earnings, funding stability, concentration risk and operational recoverability—not sector averages alone.
