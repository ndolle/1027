# Banking: geopolitical cyber threats and systemic exposure
**Assessment date: 27 October 2026 | Worldwide scope**

## 1. Executive assessment


**Geopolitics changes both the purpose of attacks and the selection of banking targets.** The material mechanisms are:

- **Espionage:** acquiring policy, financing, sanctions, transaction and relationship information.
- **Disruption:** denying access to banking services to impose costs or signal retaliation.
- **State-linked theft:** obtaining revenue through fraudulent transfers, cash-out or adjacent digital-asset operations.
- **Strategic access:** compromising infrastructure that could enable surveillance or disruption during a crisis.

**Systemic exposure is broader than direct bank compromise.** Shared communications, identity, cloud, processing and recovery infrastructure can transmit an incident across institutions. However, simultaneous targeting, a common supplier failure and self-propagating malware are different phenomena and require different evidence.

The strongest recent evidence supports targeted espionage and politically aligned denial of service. Severe multi-bank destructive disruption remains credible but should not be presented as the normal outcome of state-linked activity.


## 2. Geopolitical drivers and observed campaigns

### Russia–Ukraine: support for Ukraine influences targeting


**Observed campaign—high confidence in activity; strong attributed state link.** Microsoft’s **29 September 2026** original research describes Star Blizzard campaigns affecting more than **100 organisations**, primarily in the **US and UK**, including financial institutions supporting Ukraine politically or financially. The count covers multiple sectors, not 100 banks.

Since January, the actor expanded phishing and adopted **RedFlick**, using archive attachments, scheduled tasks and the CosmicPulse backdoor. Financial lures included an April capital-allocation/M&A discussion and an August payment-advice message. Microsoft cites US government attribution of Star Blizzard to **Russia’s FSB Centre 18**. The documented objective is espionage; the report does not establish manipulation of bank settlement systems. [1](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281771332427318888)



**Observed aligned disruption—moderate-to-high confidence, with claim limitations.** April 2026 analysis describes NoName057(16)’s DDoSia activity against financial, government and transportation infrastructure in Ukraine and countries supporting Kyiv. Its reported **1,530 successful operations** over part of late 2025–early 2026 were **attacker claims**, not independently verified bank outages. Reported Polish assessments described disruptions generally resolved within hours. Pro-Russian alignment does not, by itself, establish state tasking of each attack. [2](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281421922543599621)



**Mechanism:** financing or publicly supporting Ukraine can make an institution politically salient. Espionage targets staff and information; hacktivism targets visible service availability. Banks outside the conflict region can therefore face elevated targeting without a physical presence in Ukraine.


### Iran–Israel/US confrontation: espionage, retaliation and contested destructive claims


**Reported bank intrusion—activity documented, attribution qualified.** March 2026 reporting describes backdoors at a US bank and other organisations, publicly attributed to Iranian Seedworm/MuddyWater activity beginning in February. Crucially, GTIG’s analyst commentary **withheld judgment on the combined attribution**, distinguishing MuddyWater-associated activity from a separate cluster and noting shared signing certificates.

The evidence supports concern about persistent access and espionage. It does not establish theft of bank funds, interruption of clearing or one conclusively attributed campaign encompassing all reported victims. [3](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281298939577630726)



**Historical disruptive example—June 2025, not a new 2026 incident.** Predatory Sparrow claimed the Bank Sepah attack as retaliation for alleged military/nuclear financing. Reporting described interrupted account access, withdrawals and card payments. The claim that all bank data was destroyed remains unverified; the group’s pro-Israel positioning does not establish Israeli state direction. [4](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2280409916092973057)



**Broader destructive capability—not proof of a bank victim.** March 2026 threat analysis describes an Iranian espionage campaign against suspected Israeli targets that also deployed the SPACEHAMMER wiper during heightened tensions. The retrieved account does not identify a bank victim. [5](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281314309470420995)



**Mechanism:** banks can be targeted as financing intermediaries, symbols of an adversary’s economy or holders of intelligence. Conflict can also encourage disruptive personas to publicise claims. Attribution and damage assessment must remain separate: a verified outage does not verify every actor statement about destruction.


### North Korea: state financial gain, sanctions pressure and evolving access methods


**Established historical threat—high confidence, dated evidence.** Mandiant identifies APT38 as a North Korean regime-backed group responsible for financial theft and destructive attacks against financial institutions. Although its profile was updated in **April 2026**, its recorded last-seen activity is **March 2018**. It is therefore historical capability evidence, not proof of a newly observed 2026 bank campaign. [6](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281376905733079049)



**Recent adjacent-finance activity.** February 2026 reporting describes UNC1069 using compromised messaging accounts, meeting impersonation and troubleshooting instructions against fintech/cryptocurrency targets. This demonstrates an access technique relevant to financial-sector staff; it is not evidence that conventional bank payment systems were compromised. [7](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281239388681666567)



**Mechanism:** state-directed revenue acquisition makes financial institutions and monetisable access attractive. Sanctions exposure also complicates response and recovery transactions. Current cryptocurrency theft, historical bank heists and espionage must not be merged into a single banking incident count.


### China and strategic competition: intelligence collection and upstream access


**Observed infrastructure access—strong official attribution, indirect banking relevance.** The **7 February 2024** CISA/NSA/FBI advisory assesses that PRC state-sponsored Volt Typhoon actors were pre-positioning in US critical infrastructure for possible disruption during a major crisis or conflict. It is based on observed compromises. It does **not** establish a direct bank intrusion or activation of the assessed disruptive capability. [8](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2278977542100632155)



**Observed telecom exploitation.** Recorded Future’s **February 2025** research documents RedMike/Salt Typhoon exploitation of vulnerable devices at telecommunications providers. This establishes upstream compromise, not compromise of every bank using those carriers. [9](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2280116636146997707)



**Mechanism:** strategic intelligence can be collected through communications and technology infrastructure rather than a bank’s core network. For banks financing sensitive industries or cross-border transactions, exposed communications could reveal relationships and negotiations. A crisis-driven shift from espionage to disruption is a scenario—not an observed consequence of every Chinese-linked intrusion.

Technology restrictions and sanctions can also constrain vendor support or replacement options. That is a continuity exposure requiring supplier-specific analysis; foreign origin alone is not evidence that a product is compromised.


## 3. Disproportionately exposed subsectors and regions


The following are **exposure assessments**, not measured regional attack probabilities.

| Subsector or geography | Why exposure can be elevated | Critical dependencies |
|---|---|---|
| **Ukrainian banks and Ukraine-linked financing institutions** | Direct conflict exposure, policy intelligence value and visible support relationships | Communications, energy, identity, cross-border processing |
| **US/UK/EU institutions supporting Ukraine** | Documented espionage and aligned DDoS targeting beyond the conflict zone | Email/endpoints, public banking channels, APIs |
| **Iranian and Israeli banking ecosystems** | Retaliation, military-financing allegations and destructive targeting incentives | Card/ATM networks, communications, payment systems |
| **Gulf regional hubs** | Cross-border financial relationships and dependence on regional infrastructure | Cloud/data centres, telecoms, payments and specialist support |
| **Correspondent and transaction banks** | Sensitive cross-border payment relationships and sanctions-related information | Payment messaging, correspondents, screening, settlement access |
| **Custody, markets and investment banks** | Positions, financing and strategic transactions have intelligence value; processing is deadline-sensitive | Market data, clearing, collateral, specialist software |
| **Digital-only banks** | Customer access depends heavily on externally reachable services | DNS, telecoms, cloud, authentication and DDoS mitigation |
| **Smaller banks/BaaS ecosystems** | Shared providers and limited substitution can amplify a supplier incident | Outsourced core, processors, middleware and retained records |

Latin America, Africa and much of Asia remain exposed through global technology and payment dependencies. The available evidence does not support a complete ranking of countries or an inference that less public reporting means lower threat.


## 4. Systemic propagation and correlated disruption

### Documented correlated banking disruption


On **14 June 2026**, Iran’s banking coordination council reported a cyberattack against shared communications infrastructure used by **Bank Melli, Bank Tejarat, Bank Saderat and the Export Development Bank of Iran**. Reporting described failed payments, withdrawals and transfers. The council said it found no customer-data access or deletion. The attacker and geopolitical motive were not established.

This is evidence of correlated disruption through a common dependency—not proof of a state-linked operation. [10](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281526821012897853) [11](https://my.silobreaker.com/v2/document/5_2281528785624891400)


### Plausible systemic scenarios



| Scenario | Propagation mechanism | Consequences and evidential boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Shared cloud/identity/managed-service compromise** | Common administrative access or service failure reaches multiple institutions | Simultaneous channel loss, data exposure and impaired recovery. No state attribution should be inferred from supplier concentration alone |
| **Telecom compromise or disruption** | Carrier infrastructure affects primary and nominally “backup” connections or exposes communications | Interrupted branch/ATM/market connectivity; compromised crisis communications. Telecom espionage does not automatically establish bank-network access |
| **Energy and facilities disruption** | Power/cooling failure affects banks, data centres and telecom sites together | Backup endurance, fuel and alternate-site constraints become critical. No bank OT compromise is established here |
| **Payment/clearing access interruption** | A major participant, processor or intermediary cannot process or reconcile obligations | Queues, liquidity demands, delayed collateral and counterparty restrictions; participants can be affected without being compromised |
| **Software-supply-chain compromise** | Trusted update or integration distributes access across customers | Correlated compromise and difficult clean recovery. Several suppliers may conceal one underlying component |
| **Logistics and outsourced-operations disruption** | Cash transport, hardware maintenance or specialist support becomes unavailable | ATM/cash shortages and slower restoration. Relevance varies by location and business model |
| **Espionage combined with information operations** | Stolen documents or fabricated outage claims undermine confidence during real disruption | Customer-support overload, deposit concern and counterparty caution. Confidence effects require evidence; they are not automatic |

**Key distinction:** a supplier outage, a targeted campaign against several banks and malware propagating between networks are three different forms of correlation. Operational planning should address all three without claiming the same cause or transmission route.


## 5. Credit-relevant implications


- **Liquidity can deteriorate before losses are finalised.** Settlement interruption, collateral constraints and precautionary withdrawals can create immediate cash needs.
- **Concentration can outweigh geographic diversification.** Banks in different countries may share the same identity, software, processor or recovery provider.
- **Espionage can have delayed consequences.** Exposure of client, financing or sanctions information can create legal, commercial and subsequent fraud risks without an initial outage.
- **Conflict can weaken recovery options.** Restricted connectivity, supplier availability, travel or access to specialist personnel can make tested recovery assumptions invalid.
- **Repeated targeting can raise recurring costs.** Mitigation, monitoring, customer support and supplier redesign may reduce operating efficiency even when attacks are contained.
- **Attribution uncertainty affects decisions.** Sanctions, insurance and legal assessment may depend on facts not available during the operational crisis. Expected insurance recovery should not be assumed immediately available.

No bank-specific downgrade, deposit run, covenant breach or quantified geopolitical cyber loss is inferred from these scenarios.


## 6. Early warning indicators and escalation triggers



| Indicator | Why it matters | Evidence threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **New campaign explicitly naming banks or financing relationships** | Signals a change from broad rhetoric to relevant targeting | Corroborated telemetry or victim/official confirmation; actor posts alone are weak |
| **Phishing tied to financing, sanctions, conferences or payment advice** | Connects geopolitical interests to staff access | Campaign-specific email/endpoint evidence, including the documented Star Blizzard pattern |
| **Persistent access found in a critical supplier or management plane** | Raises common-cause and recovery risk | Supplier forensic findings and confirmed service/customer scope |
| **DDoS persists across channels or defeats failover** | Moves from publicity nuisance toward operational materiality | Measured availability, transaction failures and mitigation capacity |
| **Evidence of destructive commands or backup/identity interference** | Changes assessment from intelligence collection to potential operational destruction | Technical confirmation; not simply a wiper claim |
| **Concurrent telecom/energy failures affecting recovery locations** | May invalidate apparent redundancy | Verified route, site and provider dependencies |
| **Escalation in conflict or sanctions accompanied by technical targeting** | Can alter incentives and target selection | The political event plus relevant observed activity—not timing alone |
| **Settlement queues, failed reconciliations or restricted counterparties** | Indicates transmission into financial operations | Institution/FMI evidence rather than public-website monitoring |

### Developments that would materially change the assessment

- Confirmed state-linked compromise of a shared banking processor, payment infrastructure or recovery provider.
- Verified movement from espionage access to destructive activity.
- Repeated attacks causing missed settlement or sustained loss of minimum banking services.
- Evidence that substitute providers or recovery regions share the compromised dependency.
- Conversely, independently tested containment and recovery demonstrating service completion under the relevant scenario would reduce assessed operational severity.

**Intelligence confidence:** high for Microsoft’s observed Star Blizzard activity and the official Volt Typhoon assessment; moderate for secondary reporting on correlated Iranian disruption and hacktivist outcomes; qualified where researchers disagree on cluster attribution. Confidence is lower for undisclosed bank victims, claimed destruction and prospective systemic cascades.

**Bottom line:** geopolitical exposure should be assessed through the bank’s relationships, targeted personnel, critical services and shared dependencies—not nationality alone. The most material escalation is confirmed access that can affect transaction integrity, settlement or independent recovery.
