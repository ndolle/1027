

Agreed. For the **KNP illustration and the current Board presentation**, we'll use the **deployed 25-question toolkit shown in your screenshots**, not the future 35-question version.

That actually strengthens the use case because we are demonstrating what the **existing toolkit could already have surfaced**, with sector intelligence making those answers more decision-relevant.

The key KNP chain therefore remains:

**Q9 MFA** → initial-access exposure
**Q11 Incident Response** → ability to contain
**Q12–13 Disaster Recovery** → ability to restore
**Q14–16 Business Continuity & Cyber Resilience** → ability to keep operating
**Q18 Cyber Insurance** → financial risk transfer
**Q1/Q3 Governance** → accountability and oversight
**Q23 Risk Assessment** → whether this scenario was recognised and managed

And the central message stays:

> **The questionnaire identifies potential weaknesses; sector intelligence determines which weaknesses are particularly material for the client's sector and why; credit analysis then considers whether the client has sufficient financial resilience to absorb the resulting shock.**

For KNP specifically, that becomes a very clean chain:

**Credential weakness (Q9) → Ransomware → Recovery weakness (Q12–13) → Operational disruption (Q14–16) → Financial stress → Business viability**

This is probably the model I would now use for the **other 23 sector reports as well**: identify the handful of existing questionnaire questions that are particularly material for each sector, rather than trying to make every question equally significant.
