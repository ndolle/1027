## 🏛️ Regulatory Update

### EU pressure on Binance's market access

- European regulators are scrutinising whether Binance used a legal exemption ("reverse solicitation") to continue operating in the region  .
- The issue shows authorities are moving from consultation to direct supervision of market-access models .
- For exchanges, continuity now depends as much on licensing posture as on customer demand.
- The near-term implication is tighter review of cross-border servicing arrangements inside the EU.

### Tax treatment is becoming a frontline policy issue

- The Netherlands moved closer to a revised tax approach for crypto under its Box 3 overhaul.
- Debate over unrealized versus realized gains keeps valuation methodology and timing in focus.
- Tax design is becoming a competitive factor for retail participation and onshore custody decisions.
- Similar debates are likely to spread as governments tighten reporting and revenue collection.

### MiCA is broadening beyond its first wave

- European policymakers are pushing to widen the rulebook toward crypto lending and DeFi access points.
- The direction of travel is toward regulating gateways and intermediated risk rather than code alone.
- Lending, brokerage, and reserve management are becoming the next policy battleground after stablecoins.
- Firms built around yield and access-layer services should expect a heavier compliance build-out.

### The UK is shifting into implementation mode

- The FCA opened a crypto authorization window ahead of the UK's 2027 regime.
- The grace period gives firms a short runway to regularize operations if they want to keep serving the market.
- The UK model favours permissioned market entry over informal servicing from abroad.
- Compliance readiness is becoming a prerequisite for customer acquisition and bank relationships.

### U.S. state-level rules are filling federal gaps

- Illinois draft tax guidance now details how DeFi and stablecoins may be treated.
- The update shows U.S. policy is fragmenting through state-level interpretation while federal market-structure rules remain unfinished.
- Projects tied to swaps, staking, and stable settlement face more precise classification questions.
- Crypto firms increasingly need jurisdiction-specific tax and reporting workflows, not a single U.S. playbook.

## 📈 Cryptocurrency Outlook

### Mainstream research is turning more constructive on majors

- Citigroup raised its 12-month targets for both Bitcoin and Ethereum this week.
- The forecast upgrades reinforce that mainstream research desks still see upside after a strong third quarter.
- The call matters less as a precise target than as a signal that institutional allocators remain engaged.
- That tone supports continued preference for large-cap assets over smaller speculative names.

### Bitcoin is absorbing macro pressure without breaking structure

- Bitcoin stayed near the low-to-mid $80,000s even as a stronger U.S. dollar raised pullback risk.
- That resilience suggests macro headwinds are slowing upside momentum rather than ending the rally.
- Market tone remains constructive, but confirmation now depends on holding support instead of chasing highs.
- Near-term trading is likely to be driven by liquidity conditions and macro data more than narrative alone.

### ETF and institutional flows are still underpinning the tape

- Weekly ETF inflows remained a core source of support for Bitcoin.
- Persistent institutional demand is helping offset profit-taking after a strong quarter.
- Traditional market channels are becoming the default route for marginal crypto exposure.
- If flows remain positive, dips are more likely to be treated as re-entry opportunities than as trend reversals.

### Altcoin rotation is broadening, but selectively

- Late-quarter leadership expanded beyond Bitcoin into privacy coins and selected large-cap alts.
- Moves in names such as ZEC and NEAR show traders are looking for targeted catalysts rather than buying the whole market.
- The rotation appears strongest in liquid tokens with identifiable narratives or ecosystem developments.
- That argues for a barbell market structure: strong majors alongside event-driven alt exposure.

### Liquidity is being actively repositioned across venues

- Exchange outflows and reserve scrutiny remain important market signals after recent security shocks.
- Capital is still moving into self-custody and between venues rather than exiting crypto outright.
- That can reduce visible exchange supply while also making order books thinner during fast moves.
- The market structure therefore looks constructive, but more fragile if a fresh risk-off event hits.

## ⚠️ Technology Risk

### Third-party compromise is now exchange-critical risk

- Bitget said a third-party zero-day was behind the $387.5 million theft disclosed this week.
- The case highlights how exchange risk can sit in dependencies and transaction workflows, not only hot wallets.
- Even well-capitalised venues can face abrupt liquidity and confidence stress after a breach.
- Vendor due diligence and orchestration security are now frontline controls for large exchanges.

### Cross-chain convenience is expanding the attack surface

- NEAR Intents halted services after a roughly $3.8 million exploit.
- Cross-chain and intent-layer services continue to increase complexity across bridges, routing, and settlement logic.
- Rapid service suspensions show teams are prioritising containment, but they also expose composability risk.
- Users and allocators should expect higher scrutiny of protocols that abstract execution across multiple chains.

### Wallet-drain social engineering remains highly effective

- Attackers used Pi Coin smishing and fake setup flows to steal wallet credentials.
- These campaigns succeed because they mimic onboarding, support, or security prompts rather than overt malware.
- Seed phrase capture remains one of the fastest paths to irreversible loss.
- The exposure is greatest for retail users operating outside managed custody environments.

### Scam operations are becoming industrialised

- Reporting on romance and crypto scams points to larger, more organised fraud networks.
- The model is shifting from isolated operators to repeatable, team-based operations using scripts and playbooks.
- Crypto remains attractive because funds move quickly and recovery is difficult once assets are transferred.
- That makes prevention and payment interception more important than post-incident recovery.

### Endpoint and browser-level theft is rising

- Malicious browser extensions are impersonating trusted figures to lure crypto users into phishing and theft.
- Attackers increasingly target the user environment where wallets, extensions, and copied addresses intersect.
- Address poisoning and impersonation exploit routine habits instead of sophisticated exploitation chains.
- Device hygiene, extension control, and transaction verification are becoming as important as on-chain security.
