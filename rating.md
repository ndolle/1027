The rating represents the **risk significance of a negative answer**, not the quality of the organisation overall:

* **High:** Absence of the control could directly create material exposure or seriously impair the organisation’s ability to respond and recover. Examples include incident response, business continuity, disaster recovery and regular testing.
* **Medium:** The control is important, but the risk depends on its scope, the organisation’s exposure and available compensating controls. Examples include cyber insurance, certifications and formal roles.
* **Low:** The question provides useful supporting or contextual information, but a negative response alone would not normally justify a materially adverse risk conclusion.

A good presentation explanation would be:

> **Questions were rated according to the potential risk created by a negative response: High for controls whose absence could directly result in material impact, Medium where the impact depends on context or compensating controls, and Low for supporting indicators that inform but do not independently determine the risk conclusion.**

I would, however, challenge some of the original ratings. **MFA, vulnerability management and security patching should not automatically remain Medium**. Where they protect critical or externally exposed systems, their absence could justify a High rating. This is one reason the later framework needs risk-based weighting and evidence, rather than relying on fixed ratings attached to broad Yes/No questions.
