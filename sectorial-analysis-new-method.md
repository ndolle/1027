# Sectorial Cyber Resilience & Credit Intelligence Report

Proposed Master Slide Architecture | 24 Sectors | Senior Credit Officers

## My recommendation: 12 core slides + an evidence appendix

I would design this as a strategic intelligence briefing, not a traditional cybersecurity presentation.

The deck should be executive-grade, evidence-based, visually compelling and understandable by Senior Credit Officers who are not cybersecurity specialists.

It should answer five fundamental questions:

1. How exposed is this sector? Its inherent risk profile across the four agreed dimensions.
2. Why is it exposed? Critical dependencies, threat actors, attack pathways, geopolitics and regulatory pressures.
3. What has actually happened? Documented incidents, financial losses, business disruption and exploited weaknesses.
4. How significant could this be for a borrower? Operational and financial consequences, including potential credit implications.
5. What should the Credit Officer do with this intelligence? Contextualise questionnaire responses, engage clients and identify issues that justify further attention.

The deck should tell one coherent story rather than present isolated research findings.

Threat intelligence slides

I would apply five design rules:

- One analytical message per slide. Titles should communicate the finding, not merely label a topic.
- Charts over paragraphs. Use risk matrices, timelines, dependency maps, scenario flows and comparative metrics.
- Evidence visible. Show source references, periods and data limitations without overwhelming the slide.
- Consistent sector ratings. Use the same exposure definitions, methodology and visual scale across 24 sectors.
- Credit implications explicit. Every substantive analytical slide should answer, at least briefly, "Why does this matter to a lender?"

The imagery above illustrates design direction, not the factual content or final branding.

## 6. A final refinement I would strongly recommend

Your 12-slide architecture covers the intelligence comprehensively. But I would also introduce one cross-cutting editorial rule.

Every risk-focused slide should distinguish:

Evidence

What do we know?

Significance

Why does it matter?

Credit Lens

What should we ask?

This prevents the first ten slides from becoming technically interesting intelligence that the Credit Officer struggles to translate into a client discussion.

## Final recommendation

I would adopt this 12-slide structure as the master architecture for all 24 sectors, with seven supporting appendix modules.

I would keep the main storyline organised around four messages:

Sector exposure → Threat and resilience evidence → Operational and credit consequences → Informed client engagement.

Crucially, the report must not confuse sector-level inherent exposure with borrower-specific resilience or creditworthiness.

The real test of success is whether a Senior Credit Officer can read the executive summary in two minutes, understand the key exposures, and use Slides 10–12 to conduct a more meaningful discussion with a corporate borrower.

That is the presentation standard I would target before we begin producing all 24 decks.
