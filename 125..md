# GEOPOLITICAL NOTE | WEEK 43
**19–25 October 2026**

### AI Sovereignty: The Next Front in the Global Struggle for Power

The geopolitical competition over artificial intelligence is entering a new phase. Beyond the race for advanced chips, governments are increasingly seeking control over AI technology itself: who develops it, who can access it and who can use it for offensive purposes. ([Reuters, 16 October](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/china-proposes-adding-40-ai-biotech-solar-technologies-to-export-control-list-ce785cdad88cf12c))

**China is moving to protect AI as a strategic national asset.** Beijing has proposed export controls covering advanced AI models, autonomous agents and technologies capable of supporting cyberattacks. The move echoes Washington's restrictions on advanced semiconductor exports to China, but introduces another dimension to the rivalry: controlling the transfer of AI capabilities, not simply the hardware powering them. ([Reuters, 16 October](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/china-proposes-adding-40-ai-biotech-solar-technologies-to-export-control-list-ce785cdad88cf12c) | [White & Case, 26 October](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/china-proposes-new-export-controls-artificial-intelligence-biopharma-and-photovoltaic))

**Europe faces a growing sovereignty dilemma.** Sweden announced restrictions on government employees' direct access to ChatGPT and Claude over information-security concerns, while retaining approved access to US-based AI technology. The episode illustrates Europe's difficult position: seeking greater control over sensitive information while remaining heavily dependent on foreign technology providers. ([SVT, 23 October](https://www.svt.se/nyheter/inrikes/ai-tjanster-sparras-pa-regeringskansliet))

**Meanwhile, AI is reshaping the cyber balance of power.** Researchers have reported a campaign in which a relatively low-cost AI-assisted operation targeted and apparently compromised numerous organisations. Such capabilities could allow smaller states, criminal networks and other non-state actors to conduct operations previously requiring substantially greater resources. ([CPO Magazine, 23 October](https://www.cpomagazine.com/cyber-security/chinese-hacker-deploys-fully-automated-ai-agents-to-breach-100-companies-at-trivial-cost/) | [TechRadar, 20 October](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/chinese-hacker-hits-over-100-company-websites-using-ai-agents-deere-and-sharkninja-among-top-brands-affected-in-attack))

**Geopolitical outlook:** AI is becoming both a strategic asset to protect and a capability that can redistribute power. The emerging fault line is between countries that control advanced AI technologies and those that depend on foreign providers. This raises the prospect of a more fragmented international digital order, where technological access, economic influence and national security become increasingly intertwined. ([Reuters analysis, 28 October](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/analysischinas-new-export-curbs-draw-a-line-around-its-ai-crown-jewels-4977600))
