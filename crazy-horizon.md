Agreed. We need to move beyond today's AI agents, quantum threats and deepfakes. Those are already on the risk register.

For a 2029–2031 horizon, I would frame the poll around technologies that could fundamentally change what it means to control, trust or secure a financial institution.

## The poll: Four uncomfortable futures

**Poll: By 2030, which emerging technological shift could create the most profound systemic risk to financial services?**

**1. The Autonomous Machine Economy**\
AI agents become economic actors: creating businesses, negotiating contracts, obtaining credit, managing assets and transacting with other machines. Banks face a world where customers, counterparties and fraudsters may have no meaningful human operator.

**2. The Collapse of Digital Reality**\
Real-time synthetic identities, AI-generated histories, falsified telemetry and convincing digital evidence make it increasingly difficult to establish what is authentic. Even multiple independent verification sources may be compromised or artificially generated.

**3. The Programmable Human**\
Brain-computer interfaces, neurotechnology and AI-mediated cognition introduce a new attack surface: human perception, memory, intention and decision-making. Cybersecurity extends from protecting systems to protecting human agency itself.

**4. The Invisible Financial Infrastructure**\
Finance becomes embedded in interconnected autonomous systems, programmable assets and machine-to-machine markets. Critical financial dependencies emerge outside traditional institutional boundaries, making systemic concentration and cascading failures increasingly difficult to identify or contain.

## My preferred twist

I would close the poll with:

"Which of these scenarios would require us to fundamentally redesign our existing risk management frameworks, rather than simply strengthen our controls?"

That is a more challenging question for senior risk professionals.

The distinction matters: these are speculative but plausible stress-test scenarios, not predictions that these capabilities will exist at scale by 2030. Neurotechnology is particularly uncertain within that timeframe.

And my most provocative fifth scenario, perhaps for the discussion rather than the poll:

By 2030, the greatest cyber risk may not be malicious AI. It may be millions of perfectly functioning, independently rational autonomous systems collectively producing a financial catastrophe — with no attacker, no technical failure and no identifiable control breach.

How do you assign accountability, establish risk appetite or design effective second-line oversight for that?
