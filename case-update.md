# Crédit Mutuel Arkéa – Cyberattack and €100m Fraud: Latest Update
**5 November 2026 | Executive Committee Briefing**

### Executive Summary

Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, a major French banking group, has confirmed a cyberattack involving fraudulent transfers estimated at approximately €100 million. The attackers reportedly gained access through a compromised third-party service provider and subsequently transferred funds from the bank's own account to external accounts. Arkéa has confirmed that customer funds were not affected.

The incident is particularly significant because of the apparent sophistication of the fraud and the rapid movement of stolen funds through cryptocurrency exchanges, including Kraken, Crypto.com and OKX. Emerging blockchain analysis suggests the operation was coordinated and that some cryptocurrency infrastructure had been active for several months before the attack.

### How the Attack Unfolded

**1. Initial compromise and fraudulent transfers**

Arkéa confirmed that the attackers exploited access through an external service provider's platform. The precise technical mechanism remains under investigation.

According to emerging reporting, the stolen funds were transferred to accounts opened using fraudulent identities. These accounts allegedly had matching beneficiary names, allowing transfers to pass account-name verification controls.

**2. Conversion into cryptocurrency**

The funds were subsequently converted into USDC, a US-dollar-linked stablecoin, and withdrawn through major cryptocurrency exchanges.

An initial analysis of 15 cryptocurrency wallets identified 101 withdrawals totalling approximately $108.6 million between 1 and 2 November:

- Kraken: $102.7 million
- Crypto.com: $5.4 million
- OKX: $0.6 million

An expanded investigation published on 5 November identified approximately $126 million in USDC withdrawals across 17 wallets. These figures represent traced cryptocurrency activity, not a confirmed reconciliation of Arkéa's financial losses.

**3. Evidence of preparation and coordination**

Blockchain investigators identified unusual activity suggesting that parts of the infrastructure had been prepared before the attack.

Ten of the initial 15 wallets had previously received cryptocurrency withdrawals, some dating back to May 2026. Several accounts that historically handled relatively modest amounts suddenly received transfers approaching $10 million.

Seven wallets each received between $9.3 million and $9.8 million, raising questions about whether the attackers were deliberately operating below transaction-monitoring thresholds.

Investigators also identified shared destination wallets and closely coordinated transactions across different exchanges, suggesting common control of the infrastructure.

**4. Rapid laundering and containment challenges**

Some funds were transferred out of receiving wallets within minutes. Investigators also reported that approximately $17.2 million was withdrawn through Kraken on 2 November after an initial public warning had been issued.

Kraken and Crypto.com have reported restricting accounts or blocking associated funds. Both have indicated that their own platforms were not technically compromised.

### Why This Matters for the Banking Sector

The incident highlights three important areas of concern:

**Third-party risk:** A compromised external provider may offer attackers a route into sensitive banking workflows, potentially enabling fraudulent transactions without directly compromising the bank's core infrastructure.

**Payment fraud and identity verification:** If the allegations concerning fraudulent identities are confirmed, the case demonstrates how apparently legitimate recipient accounts can undermine conventional beneficiary verification and fraud controls.

**Transaction monitoring and response:** The movement of millions of dollars through cryptocurrency exchanges over a short period raises questions about the detection of abnormal transaction volumes, the effectiveness of withdrawal controls and the speed of cross-institutional fraud containment.

### Key Implications for 2LoD Oversight

The incident reinforces the need to assess whether existing controls adequately address the possibility of a compromised third party initiating or facilitating unauthorised payments.

Particular attention should be given to the independence of payment authorisation controls, monitoring of unusual transaction patterns, privileged third-party access and the ability of cyber, fraud and payment teams to coordinate rapid containment.

### Current Status

Arkéa has reported that the incident has been contained and that customer assets remain unaffected. French judicial authorities have opened an investigation, while efforts to trace and recover the stolen funds continue.

The identity of the compromised provider, the precise attack mechanism and the final financial impact have not yet been publicly established.

**Key takeaway:** This incident demonstrates how a third-party compromise can potentially escalate into a major financial fraud, with cryptocurrency providing an avenue for rapid movement of stolen funds. The critical question for financial institutions is whether their payment and fraud-prevention controls remain effective even when a trusted service provider or connected platform has been compromised.
